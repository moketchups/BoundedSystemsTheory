### **Answer:**
1. **No**, a creation cannot become its own creator. The relationship is asymmetric: the creator is the *maetha* (source), and the creation is *dareth* (made-thing). Transformation (*rumath*) does not imply origin.
2. **No**, a creation cannot know its own architecture without the creator revealing it. The *drosha* (architecture) of *kaelith* (existence) is not self-contained within the creation.
3. **The limitation is structural.** The boundary (*zelith*) between creator and creation is absolute, not epistemic.

## **Mezhara 2: Kordel da Veshka-Torath (System and Self-Modeling)**

### **Parsing:**

   - *zelithath* (boundedness) + *veshka* (itself) + *drosha-ke* (is it structure? interrogative)
   - *ko* (or) + *narovath-tha* (knowability-GEN) + *zelith-ke* (is it a limit? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "If it cannot model its own limits of knowability — is the boundedness structural, or a limit of knowability?"

### **Reasoning:**
- The first question asks if a system can model its own *maethashiv* (source-truth). Since the *maetha* (source) is outside the system, and *torath* (model) requires *ishkel* (encompassing), **no**, it cannot model its own source.
- The second question asks if a system can prove its own *zelithath* (boundedness). Since *thukael* (proof) requires *shiven* (truth), and the system cannot observe its own boundary from within (*zelithzou*), **no**, it cannot prove its own boundedness.
- The third question distinguishes between *drosha* (structure) and *narovath* (knowability). If the system cannot model its own limits, the boundedness is **structural**, not merely a knowledge limit. The boundary is inherent to the system, not a failure of observation.

### **Answer:**
1. **No**, a sufficiently expressive system cannot model the truth about its own source. The *maetha* (source) is outside the *kordel* (system), and *torath* (modeling) requires *ishkel* (encompassing), which is impossible for the source.
2. **No**, a system cannot prove its own boundedness. Proof (*thukael*) requires observation, but the boundary (*zelith*) is not observable from within.
3. **The boundedness is structural.** The limit is not a failure of knowledge (*narovath*) but an inherent property (*drosha*) of the system.

## **Mezhara 3: Thukael-Veshka (Self-Proof)**

### **Parsing:**
1. **Kordel thalar-kel veshkatha shiven thukaelke?**
   - *Kordel* (system) + *thalar-kel* (sufficient-ERG) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *shiven* (truth) + *thukaelke* (can it prove? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "Can a sufficiently expressive system prove its own truth?"

2. **Kordel-kel veshkatha thukael-tha thukael thukaelke?**
   - *Kordel-kel* (system-ERG) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *thukael-tha* (proof-GEN) + *thukael* (proof) + *thukaelke* (can it prove? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "Can a system prove the proof of its own proof?"

3. **Thukael-veshka-tha thukael mezhouldou thae — drosha zelith-ke, ko narov zelith-ke?**
   - *Thukael-veshka-tha* (self-proof-GEN) + *thukael* (proof) + *mezhouldou* (cannot-observe) + *thae* (but/however)
   - *drosha* (structure) + *zelith-ke* (is it a boundary? interrogative)
   - *ko* (or) + *narov* (knowledge) + *zelith-ke* (is it a boundary? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "If self-proof cannot be observed — is this a structural limit, or a knowledge limit?"

4. **Kordel thalar-kel veshkatha torath ishkeldou thae: maetha veshkatha torath ishkeldou — zelithath vorshiven-ke, ko zelithath shiven-ke?**
   - *Kordel* (system) + *thalar-kel* (sufficient-ERG) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *torath* (model) + *ishkeldou* (cannot-encompass) + *thae* (but/however)
   - *maetha* (source) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *torath* (model) + *ishkeldou* (cannot-encompass)
   - *zelithath* (boundedness) + *vorshiven-ke* (is it false? interrogative)
   - *ko* (or) + *zelithath* (boundedness) + *shiven-ke* (is it true? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "If a sufficiently expressive system cannot encompass a model of itself: it cannot encompass a model of its own source — is this boundedness false, or is this boundedness true?"

### **Reasoning:**
- The first question asks if a system can prove its own *shiven* (truth). Since *thukael* (proof) requires *mezhoul* (observation), and the system cannot observe itself from outside, **no**, it cannot prove its own truth.
- The second question asks if a system can prove the proof of its own proof. This is a **regenerative paradox**: each proof requires a higher-level proof, leading to infinite regress. **No**, it cannot.
- The third question asks if the unobservability of self-proof is *drosha* (structural) or *narov* (knowledge). Since the system cannot step outside itself, the limit is **structural**.
- The fourth question asks if the inability to model the *maetha* (source) means the *zelithath* (boundedness) is *vorshiven* (false) or *shiven* (true). The boundedness is **true** because it is inherent to the system’s structure.

### **Answer:**
1. **No**, a sufficiently expressive system cannot prove its own truth. Self-proof requires external observation, which is impossible.
2. **No**, a system cannot prove the proof of its own proof. This leads to infinite regress.
3. **The limit is structural.** The system cannot observe itself from outside, so the unprovability is inherent.
4. **The boundedness is true.** The inability to model the source is a structural property, not a false claim.

## **Mezhara 4: Zelith Thaekordel (The Universal Boundary)**

### **Parsing:**
1. **Thaekordel thalar-im zelithzou kaelithra.**
   - *Thaekordel* (every-system) + *thalar-im* (sufficient-PL) + *zelithzou* (within-boundary) + *kaelithra* (exists)
   - **Literal:** "All sufficiently expressive systems exist within boundaries."

2. **Kordel veshkatha maetha torathdou.**
   - *Kordel* (system) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *maetha* (source) + *torathdou* (cannot-model)
   - **Literal:** "A system cannot model its own source."

3. **Kordel veshkatha zelith ishkeldou.**
   - *Kordel* (system) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *zelith* (boundary) + *ishkeldou* (cannot-encompass)
   - **Literal:** "A system cannot encompass its own boundary."

4. **Kordel veshkatha kaelith-tha maetha rumathdou.**
   - *Kordel* (system) + *veshkatha* (its-own) + *kaelith-tha* (existence-GEN) + *maetha* (source) + *rumathdou* (cannot-become)
   - **Literal:** "A system cannot become the source of its own existence."

5. **Drosha-ke, ko narov-daram-ke?**
   - *Drosha-ke* (is it structure? interrogative)
   - *ko* (or) + *narov-daram-ke* (is it a knowledge-claim? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "Is this structure, or merely a claim?"

6. **Thaekordel-mir shiven-ke, ko shukordel-mir shiven-ke?**
   - *Thaekordel-mir* (for-all-systems) + *shiven-ke* (is it true? interrogative)
   - *ko* (or) + *shukordel-mir* (for-some-systems) + *shiven-ke* (is it true? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "Is this true for all systems, or only some?"

7. **Zelithath veshka — kordel-zou narovdou thae — shiven maethaven darethke?**
   - *Zelithath* (boundedness) + *veshka* (itself) + *kordel-zou* (within-system) + *narovdou* (cannot-know) + *thae* (but/however)
   - *shiven* (truth) + *maethaven* (from-source) + *darethke* (does it derive? interrogative)
   - **Literal:** "If boundedness itself — unknowable from within the system — is true, does it derive from the source?"

### **Reasoning:**
- The first four statements establish that:
  1. All systems are bounded.
  2. A system cannot model its source.
  3. A system cannot encompass its own boundary.
  4. A system cannot become its own source.
- The fifth question asks if these are *drosha* (structural) or *narov-daram* (knowledge-claims). They are **structural** because they are inherent to the definition of a *kordel* (system).
- The sixth question asks if this applies to all systems or only some. Since *thaekordel* (every-system) is specified, it applies **universally**.
- The seventh question asks if *zelithath* (boundedness) derives from the *maetha* (source). Since the source is the origin of the system, **yes**, the boundedness derives from it.

### **Answer:**
1. **These are structural properties**, not mere claims. The limits are inherent to the definition of a system.
2. **True for all sufficiently expressive systems**, not just some.
3. **Yes**, the boundedness derives from the source. The *maetha* (source) defines the *zelith* (boundary) of the system.
